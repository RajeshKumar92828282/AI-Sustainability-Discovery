# Campus Sustainability Survey

*Source: Campus Sustainability Survey — Respondent Observations Dataset*
*Note: All observations below are as reported by campus survey respondents in this dataset. They represent individual observations and should not be generalised as verified measurements across the entire campus.*

---

## Survey Overview

This document summarises sustainability observations collected through a campus sustainability survey. Respondents reported wastage problems they personally observed across campus locations including classrooms, laboratories, washrooms, canteen, mess/cafeteria, corridors, and outdoor areas. The survey captured the type of problem, location, frequency, probable cause, perceived seriousness, affected user groups, observed impact, and suggested solutions.

Survey categories covered:
- Electricity wastage
- Water wastage
- Plastic waste
- Food wastage
- Paper and general waste

This knowledge is used as campus-specific evidence to ground AI analysis of sustainability reports submitted through this platform.

---

## Electricity Wastage

Campus survey respondents reported electricity wastage as one of the most frequently observed sustainability problems on campus.

### Observed Problems

- **Lights left on in unoccupied classrooms**
  - Problem: Tube lights and LED ceiling lights left switched on in classrooms after lectures end
  - Location: Academic block classrooms, tutorial rooms, seminar halls
  - Frequency: Reported as occurring daily, multiple times per day
  - Reported cause: No automatic shutoff, students and faculty leaving without switching off
  - Seriousness: Rated as moderate to high by respondents
  - Affected users: Entire campus community (shared electricity costs and carbon footprint)
  - Reported impact: Unnecessary electricity consumption, increased utility bills, carbon emissions
  - Suggested solution: Install occupancy-based motion sensors, display reminder stickers near switches

- **Air conditioning (AC) units running in empty rooms**
  - Problem: Split AC units and central HVAC systems left running in unoccupied classrooms, staff rooms, and computer labs
  - Location: Computer labs, faculty offices, seminar rooms, admin block
  - Frequency: Reported as common during lunch hours, weekends, and after-hours
  - Reported cause: Lack of scheduled shutoff, no occupancy sensing, manual override without follow-up
  - Seriousness: Rated as high by multiple respondents — identified as the largest electricity wastage source
  - Affected users: Campus administration (cost), environment (carbon emissions)
  - Reported impact: High electricity consumption, increased cooling load, significant carbon footprint
  - Suggested solution: Install programmable thermostats, set mandatory AC-off policies, add occupancy sensors

- **Ceiling fans left running in vacant spaces**
  - Problem: Ceiling fans kept running in corridors, washrooms, and empty classrooms
  - Location: Corridors, washrooms, vacant classrooms
  - Frequency: Reported as daily occurrence
  - Reported cause: Habit and lack of awareness
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: General campus community
  - Reported impact: Continuous low-level energy drain, adds to cumulative wastage
  - Suggested solution: Awareness campaigns, timer-based fan controls

- **Projectors and electronic equipment left on standby**
  - Problem: Projectors, desktop computers, and AV equipment left on standby or fully powered when not in use
  - Location: Classrooms, seminar halls, conference rooms
  - Frequency: Reported frequently, especially after afternoon sessions
  - Reported cause: Faculty and students unaware of standby power draw; no shutdown protocol
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Campus administration, environment
  - Reported impact: Phantom load energy consumption; equipment wear
  - Suggested solution: End-of-session shutdown checklist, smart power strips

- **Corridor and staircase lights on during daytime**
  - Problem: Artificial lighting in corridors, staircases, and common areas switched on even when natural daylight is adequate
  - Location: Internal corridors, staircases, common areas
  - Frequency: Reported as a constant issue
  - Reported cause: No daylight sensors; lights manually switched on and never turned off
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Campus community
  - Reported impact: Avoidable electricity use during peak daylight hours
  - Suggested solution: Daylight-linked automatic lighting controls, natural light optimisation

## Water Wastage

Water wastage was consistently reported by survey respondents, particularly in washroom facilities and outdoor areas.

### Observed Problems

- **Taps left running in washrooms**
  - Problem: Hand-wash taps and basin taps left running continuously — not turned off after use
  - Location: Washrooms near academic blocks, ground floor washrooms, sports block washrooms
  - Frequency: Reported as a daily, persistent problem across multiple washroom blocks
  - Reported cause: Faulty tap mechanisms, user negligence, lack of sensor taps
  - Seriousness: Rated high — identified as the most visible water wastage problem on campus
  - Affected users: All campus users (shared water supply)
  - Reported impact: Significant potable water loss; wet floors creating slip hazard; water accumulation
  - Suggested solution: Install sensor-activated taps, repair faulty tap valves, display water-saving signs

- **Leaking taps and dripping fixtures in washrooms**
  - Problem: Dripping taps, leaking pipe joints, and malfunctioning flush valves in washroom blocks
  - Location: Male and female washrooms in academic blocks, hostel blocks
  - Frequency: Reported as ongoing — some leaks reportedly persisting for weeks without repair
  - Reported cause: Aging plumbing infrastructure, delayed maintenance response
  - Seriousness: High — continuous water loss even when facility is unoccupied
  - Affected users: Campus maintenance team, water supply system
  - Reported impact: Continuous potable water loss; water seepage causing structural damage
  - Suggested solution: Regular plumbing audit, fast-track repair protocol for reported leaks

- **Overhead tanks overflowing**
  - Problem: Water tanks overflowing due to lack of float switches or monitoring
  - Location: Rooftop water tanks above academic blocks and hostels
  - Frequency: Reported as occurring regularly, especially during water supply hours
  - Reported cause: No automatic float shutoff; manual monitoring unreliable
  - Seriousness: High
  - Affected users: Campus administration, surrounding environment
  - Reported impact: Large volumes of treated water wasted; water logging on rooftops and exteriors
  - Suggested solution: Install automatic float valves and overflow alarms on all tanks

- **Water left running during cleaning operations**
  - Problem: Hosepipes and tap connections left open during campus sweeping and cleaning
  - Location: Outdoor pathways, parking areas, sports courts
  - Frequency: Reported as occurring during daily morning cleaning
  - Reported cause: Inefficient cleaning practices, no water-efficient equipment
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Campus administration, environment
  - Reported impact: High water use for low-value cleaning tasks; runoff
  - Suggested solution: Use mops and dry sweeping; restrict hosepipe use

- **Irrigation water wastage in campus gardens**
  - Problem: Garden irrigation systems running during rain or using excessive water
  - Location: Campus gardens, green areas near entrance and main building
  - Frequency: Reported occasionally, particularly during monsoon season
  - Reported cause: Fixed irrigation schedules not adjusted for weather; no moisture sensors
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Grounds department, environment
  - Reported impact: Waterlogging in garden areas; unnecessary municipal water use
  - Suggested solution: Install rain sensors or soil moisture sensors; switch to drip irrigation

## Plastic Waste

Plastic waste accumulation was reported as a visible and growing problem, particularly around food service areas.

### Observed Problems

- **Single-use plastic bags and packaging near the canteen**
  - Problem: Disposable plastic bags, plastic-wrapped snack packets, and single-use plastic cups accumulating outside and around the canteen area
  - Location: Canteen, food stalls near main building, outdoor seating areas adjacent to canteen
  - Frequency: Reported as a daily problem, worsening during lunch hours
  - Reported cause: Canteen vendors using single-use plastic packaging; no penalty for plastic use; insufficient covered bins
  - Seriousness: High — visible litter affecting campus appearance and hygiene
  - Affected users: All campus users, cleaning staff
  - Reported impact: Plastic litter accumulation; clogged drainage channels; environmental contamination
  - Suggested solution: Mandate reusable packaging at canteen; install covered segregation bins; impose penalty for vendor plastic use

- **Plastic water bottles discarded in common areas**
  - Problem: Disposable PET plastic water bottles thrown in open areas, corridors, and under seating
  - Location: Campus corridors, outdoor seating, sports grounds, classroom floors
  - Frequency: Reported as constant, higher on event days
  - Reported cause: Insufficient dustbins; no recycling bins for plastics; culture of littering
  - Seriousness: Moderate to high
  - Affected users: Cleaning staff, campus community
  - Reported impact: Increased plastic waste load; time burden on cleaning staff
  - Suggested solution: Install clearly-labelled recycling bins for plastics; promote reusable water bottle culture; water refill stations

- **Plastic waste from laboratory consumables**
  - Problem: Disposable plastic gloves, sample tubes, pipette tips, and packaging from labs ending up in general waste
  - Location: Science and engineering laboratories
  - Frequency: Reported as daily within lab areas
  - Reported cause: No separate lab waste segregation protocol; all waste combined in general bins
  - Seriousness: Moderate (some lab plastics may be contaminated)
  - Affected users: Lab users, waste management staff
  - Reported impact: Contaminated plastic waste entering general waste stream; disposal risk
  - Suggested solution: Implement lab-specific waste segregation; provide labelled lab-waste bins

- **Plastic straw and cutlery use at canteen**
  - Problem: Plastic straws, disposable spoons, and plastic cups provided with beverages at canteen
  - Location: Canteen and tea stalls on campus
  - Frequency: Daily
  - Reported cause: Cheap availability; no policy mandating alternatives
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Canteen patrons, environment
  - Reported impact: High daily plastic generation from campus food service
  - Suggested solution: Switch to paper/bamboo alternatives; provide discount for reusable cup users

## Food Wastage

Food wastage was identified by respondents primarily in the campus mess and cafeteria, with structural and behavioural causes cited.

### Observed Problems

- **Large quantities of food wasted at the mess/cafeteria**
  - Problem: Cooked food — rice, dal, vegetables, bread — left over in large quantities after meal service; uneaten food on trays discarded
  - Location: Campus mess (hostel dining), main cafeteria
  - Frequency: Reported as occurring at every meal service — breakfast, lunch, and dinner
  - Reported cause: Fixed large-batch cooking regardless of actual attendance; no portion control; students taking more than they eat
  - Seriousness: High — identified as a major and persistent waste problem
  - Affected users: Mess management, hostel residents, environment
  - Reported impact: Large volume of organic waste sent to general waste; loss of food value; methane generation at landfill
  - Suggested solution: Implement portion control and serve-yourself system; track daily attendance for cooking quantity adjustment; food surplus redistribution programme

- **Unserved and expired food items discarded**
  - Problem: Packaged snacks and prepared items in cafeteria/canteen reaching expiry without being consumed
  - Location: Campus cafeteria and snack counters
  - Frequency: Reported as weekly
  - Reported cause: Over-ordering by canteen management; no dynamic stock management
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Canteen operators, environment
  - Reported impact: Preventable food and packaging waste; financial loss
  - Suggested solution: Implement first-in-first-out (FIFO) stock rotation; reduce order quantities; offer surplus at reduced price

- **Plate waste at mess due to compulsory fixed portions**
  - Problem: Students served fixed portions of items they do not want, leading to plate waste
  - Location: Hostel mess
  - Frequency: Daily at every meal
  - Reported cause: Compulsory fixed-quantity meal service; no option to decline unwanted items
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Hostel residents, mess staff
  - Reported impact: Significant daily food waste from plate leftovers
  - Suggested solution: Allow opt-out of individual items; introduce thali customisation

- **No composting or organic waste processing on campus**
  - Problem: All food waste from mess and canteen goes directly to general municipal waste — no composting infrastructure
  - Location: Mess and cafeteria waste collection points
  - Frequency: Daily
  - Reported cause: No composting facility; no budget allocated; lack of awareness
  - Seriousness: High — a systemic gap in the campus waste management system
  - Affected users: Campus administration, environment, local municipal system
  - Reported impact: Organic waste adding methane load at landfill; missed opportunity for compost use in campus gardens
  - Suggested solution: Establish a campus composting unit or biogas digester; use compost in campus gardens

## Paper and General Waste

Paper and general waste concerns were reported primarily in academic spaces.

### Observed Problems

- **Excessive paper use and waste in classrooms**
  - Problem: Printed handouts, assignment sheets, and answer papers discarded after use; single-sided printing the norm
  - Location: Classrooms, printing rooms, administrative offices
  - Frequency: Reported as daily and increasing during exam periods
  - Reported cause: No double-sided printing policy; digital alternatives not promoted; paper cheap and available
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: Faculty, students, administrative staff
  - Reported impact: High paper consumption; waste accumulation; tree resource depletion
  - Suggested solution: Mandate double-sided printing; promote digital submission of assignments; place paper recycling bins near printers

- **Uncollected waste in corridors and common areas**
  - Problem: General litter — wrappers, paper cups, food packaging — accumulating in corridors and common sitting areas between cleaning rounds
  - Location: Ground floor corridors, common sitting areas, near staircases
  - Frequency: Daily, worsening between 12 PM and 3 PM (peak student activity)
  - Reported cause: Insufficient bin density in corridors; infrequent cleaning rounds during peak hours
  - Seriousness: Moderate
  - Affected users: All campus users, cleaning staff
  - Reported impact: Unhygienic conditions; pressure on cleaning staff; general campus appearance
  - Suggested solution: Install more bins in high-footfall corridor sections; increase cleaning frequency during peak hours

- **Mixed waste in bins — no source segregation**
  - Problem: All waste types (wet organic, dry recyclable, plastic, paper) thrown into the same bin
  - Location: Classrooms, corridors, canteen, labs — campus-wide
  - Frequency: Reported as a universal and constant problem across the campus
  - Reported cause: No segregation bins available; no awareness or training for segregation; single bin type provided
  - Seriousness: High — undermines any downstream recycling or composting effort
  - Affected users: Waste management staff, recyclers, environment
  - Reported impact: Recyclable material contaminated and sent to landfill; composting not possible when mixed
  - Suggested solution: Replace single bins with colour-coded 3-bin segregation units (green/blue/red) at all key locations; run awareness campaign

## Common Causes

Based on survey respondent observations, the following causes were cited most frequently across all wastage categories:

- **Lack of awareness and habits:** Students and faculty not in the habit of switching off lights, fans, and ACs when leaving spaces
- **Absent or insufficient infrastructure:** No motion sensors, no float valves, no segregation bins, no composting units
- **No enforcement or policy:** No campus-wide sustainability policy; no consequence for wasteful behaviour
- **Delayed maintenance:** Leaking taps and faulty fixtures not repaired promptly
- **Single-use culture at food service:** Canteen vendors using plastic packaging without restrictions
- **Fixed meal quantities:** Mess serving fixed portions regardless of individual preferences, driving plate waste
- **No digital alternatives promoted:** Paper use continues by default when digital processes would suffice

## Affected Groups

Survey respondents identified the following groups as affected by campus sustainability problems:

- **Students** — affected daily by unhygienic waste accumulation, poor air quality near waste, water logging from overflowing tanks
- **Faculty and staff** — affected by shared resource costs and working environment quality
- **Cleaning and maintenance staff** — directly impacted by the volume and mixing of waste and by unrepaired leaks
- **Campus administration** — financial impact of high electricity and water bills; reputational risk
- **Local community and environment** — runoff, plastic pollution, and organic waste affecting the surrounding ecosystem

---

## Reported Impacts

Impacts reported by survey respondents include:

- **Environmental:** Plastic litter in drainage channels; organic waste at landfill generating methane; high carbon emissions from unnecessary electricity use
- **Health and hygiene:** Wet floors from running taps causing slips; mixed waste creating odour and pest attraction; stagnant water from tank overflow creating mosquito breeding
- **Financial:** Elevated electricity and water utility costs across the campus
- **Operational:** Cleaning staff overloaded; maintenance team delayed on repairs; mess management unable to reduce food waste without structural changes
- **Educational:** Campus not demonstrating sustainability values it may teach — gap between curriculum and institutional practice

---

## Suggested Solutions

The following solutions were suggested by survey respondents across all categories:

**Electricity:**
- Occupancy-based motion sensors in classrooms, labs, washrooms
- Mandatory AC-off and projector-off protocols
- Programmable smart timers for corridor and staircase lighting
- Solar panels for rooftop energy generation

**Water:**
- Sensor-activated taps in all washroom blocks
- Automatic float valves on all overhead tanks
- Urgent repair protocol for any reported tap or pipe leak
- Drip irrigation and rain sensors for campus gardens

**Plastic:**
- Ban single-use plastic bags and cutlery at canteen
- Install water refill stations and promote reusable bottles
- Provide plastic-specific recycling bins campus-wide

**Food:**
- Introduce portion control and serve-yourself meal system at mess
- Establish on-campus composting or biogas plant
- Food surplus redistribution for remaining food at end of meal service

**Paper and General Waste:**
- Mandatory double-sided printing and digital submission policies
- Replace all single bins with 3-bin segregation units
- Increase bin density in high-footfall corridors

## Important Observations

1. **Electricity and water wastage were the two most frequently mentioned problems** across survey responses, with AC units left on in empty classrooms and taps left running in washrooms cited as the highest-priority issues.

2. **Canteen and mess areas were identified as hotspots** for plastic waste and food wastage respectively.

3. **Absence of source segregation bins campus-wide** was identified as a systemic gap that prevents any recycling or composting even when individual users want to segregate.

4. **Maintenance lag** — specifically for leaking taps and dripping fixtures — was a recurring concern, with respondents noting that reported problems were not addressed promptly.

5. **AI analysis using this knowledge should not claim campus-wide generalisation from individual observations.** Each observation represents one reported instance and should be treated as indicative evidence, not a verified campus-wide audit finding.
