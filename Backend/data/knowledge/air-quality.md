# Ambient & Indoor Air Quality Management

## Problem Identification & Impact
Poor air quality caused by particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), vehicular exhaust, dust, and toxic fumes impacts public health, respiratory safety, and overall well-being. Idle vehicles near campus gates and uncontained construction dust worsen local air quality.

## Root Causes
- Vehicle idling at pickup points, entrances, and parking areas.
- Open burning of dry leaves, yard trimmings, or plastic waste.
- Poor indoor ventilation combined with lack of air filtration systems.
- Uncovered soil, unpaved roads, or ongoing construction work emitting fugitive dust.

## Actionable Mitigation Strategies
1. **No-Idling Zones:** Enforce strict "No Vehicle Idling" zones near entrances, school gates, and residential quarters.
2. **Dust Suppression:** Implement water spraying on unpaved pathways and cover construction materials with tarpaulins.
3. **Green Buffers:** Plant high-absorption evergreen trees and hedges (such as Neem, Bamboo, and Snake plants) along perimeter fences to act as natural bio-filters.
4. **Monitoring & Ban:** Prohibit all open biomass burning and deploy IoT low-cost particulate matter sensors for real-time Air Quality Index (AQI) monitoring.

- **SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being):** Target 3.9 (Substantially reduce illnesses from hazardous chemicals and air pollution).
- **SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities):** Target 11.6 (Pay special attention to air quality and municipal waste management).
